DOSy++ — browser host

An ADC (Direct Connect) client for MS-DOS. This is the real DJGPP binary, on an emulated 486, with a packet driver and Watt-32 under it — not a reimplementation in JavaScript.
Idle.

Network

A browser tab cannot open a TCP socket, and WebAssembly does not change that — it reaches the network through the same JavaScript APIs, and none of them do TCP. So there are two ways to give the DOS guest a network, and the first needs nothing at all.

In this tab runs the other end of the wire in JavaScript: the Wisp stream the guest opens is handed to an ADC hub and a serving peer a few hundred lines away (wisp.mjs, adchub.mjs). Real protocol, real files, real hashes — and nothing leaves your browser, so no hub on the internet is involved. It is your own hub: nobody else is in it.

Running a hub of your own instead

Pick through a relay and point it at wisprelay.py on your machine, which can reach whatever hub you tell it to — including an adcs:// one, since it terminates the TLS the DOS binary does not have. That is also how two people end up in the same hub, which the in-tab one cannot do. 

curl -fsSL ./relay/demo.sh | sh

Game MIDI

A DOS game that offers you "Roland MPU-401" writes MIDI notes to port 330h and expects a synthesiser on the other end. v86 answers the game's probe and then throws every note away — so the game detects a Roland and plays silence. This page takes the notes off the port itself and plays them.

off

A MIDI device is the better sound and costs no download: the notes leave the browser and go to whatever you have — a Roland module, a keyboard, a software synth, Windows' own GS Wavetable. Needs Web MIDI, which Safari does not have.

"Use my own SoundFont" takes any .sf2, .sf3 or .dls file from your disk — an SC-55 or MT-32 set you already own works, and skips the 40MB download. The file is read in the browser and never leaves your machine. Nothing of that kind is bundled here: those sets are sample recordings of Roland hardware and are not ours to ship.

The demo

Download a game over Direct Connect, on DOS, in a browser tab, then unzip it and play it. The buttons type for you — or click into the screen and type it yourself.

  1. Boot. FreeDOS comes up, loads the RTL8029 packet driver and starts DOSy++ against the hub. It asks what to call you — type a handle, or press ↵ for WebNode. The tune is a ProTracker module through the emulated Sound Blaster.
  2. Find a game. Door 3 is the file areas. BOOM is Doom — the GPL engine with a free WAD, 7.8MB — and its music plays if you pressed Play it here above. BLKDROP is 109KB if you would rather not wait.
  3. Queue it. ↵ on a result queues it; door 4 watches it arrive and verify against the hash the hub advertised.
  4. Leave the client. It drops you at a DOS prompt, and what you downloaded is sitting in C:\ as BOOM.ZIP.
  5. Unzip it and play. PLAY does both: UNZIP it, set its music to the MPU-401 this page listens to, and run what comes out. Games that want a mouse have one — the boot loads CTMOUSE, and v86's mouse is yours once you click into the screen.
Unzipping it by hand, and what PLAY is doing for you

UNZIP.EXE is on C:\ — it comes from the FreeDOS unzip package, which the demo's own share also carries. Doom unpacks to about 19MB, which a 486 in a browser tab does in a few seconds. 

DIR *.ZIP                      what you downloaded
UNZIP -o -q BOOM.ZIP           unpack it (-o overwrites, -q is quiet)
COPY BOOMMIDI.CFG GAMES\BOOM\BOOM.CFG    music through the MPU-401
CD GAMES\BOOM
BOOM                           play it
CD \                           and back out afterwards

That COPY is the line that matters for sound. Boom asks Allegro to autodetect its music device, and inside v86 autodetect finds the Sound Blaster and picks an FM chip the emulator does not implement — so Doom runs perfectly and plays silence. BOOMMIDI.CFG is the package's own config with music_card = 6, which is the MPU-401, which is the port this page is listening to. PLAY copies it for you.

To get back to the client afterwards: DOSYPP 10.77.0.1 — the address means "the hub", whether that is the one in this tab or one a relay points at.

What the script does, if you would rather not pipe curl to bash 

  

    The first two are the relay and the hub. The third is the share the hub
    serves: free DOS software from the FreeDOS repositories, with its
    hashes precomputed — the client verifies every download against
    them, and Python has no Tiger to compute them with.
  

  

    --map is the trick that makes this work without DNS: v86
    hands the relay the dotted quad the guest dialled, as a hostname, so
    10.77.0.1 means "the hub" and 10.77.0.2 means
    "the node with the files". The DOS side never learns a real address.
  






  How this works, and what it cannot do
  

    v86 emulates a PC with an ne2k NIC (RTL8029). The guest
    loads an ordinary packet driver, Watt-32 sees an ordinary NIC, and
    DOSy++ runs unmodified — the same binary as real
    hardware. Guest address 192.168.86.100, gateway
    192.168.86.1, both static: v86 answers DHCP in
    JavaScript but Watt-32 does not take its offer.
  

  

    Incoming connections. Wisp carries outbound streams only, so the
    guest is passive: it cannot be dialled and says so in its INF. A peer
    that wants a file from it sends a CTM and the guest does the dialling.
    That is also why downloading works at all.
  

  

    ADCS. There is no TLS in the DOS binary. The relay is the TLS
    endpoint: --map 10.77.0.1=hub.example.net:2780:tls. The
    relay therefore sees that traffic in the clear, which is an argument
    for running it yourself and not an argument for hosting one here.
  

  

    Sound. PCM through the emulated Sound Blaster's DMA, which is
    our own mixer feeding it. v86 does not implement the DMA controller's
    count register, so the mixer times the ring from the clock instead and
    says so in door 7. There is no OPL either, so FM music is silent here
    whatever a game reports — on real hardware the same binary
    drives a real card.
  

  

    Game MIDI. v86's MPU-401 answers a game's probe at 330h and
    discards every note, which is worse than having none: the game picks
    Roland and goes quiet. So this page wraps that I/O port, reassembles
    the stream (running status, sysex and all) and plays it through a
    SoundFont synthesiser on the audio thread. The byte stream and the
    synthesis are both checked headlessly by make
    test-v86-midi, which renders what you would hear and measures
    it; what no rig here can check is the last hop to your speakers.
  

  

    Persistence. Save session writes the whole machine state to
    IndexedDB, so the identity, the hash cache and the favourites survive
    a reload. It is your browser's storage: nothing leaves the tab.
  

  

    What this page is not. It is not a hub, not a relay and not a
    tracker. It serves static files; everything else runs on your machine.
